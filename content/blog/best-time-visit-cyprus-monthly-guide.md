## When Should You Visit Cyprus?

It is one of the questions we hear most frequently at JetSet Travel: "When is the best time to visit Cyprus?" The answer depends entirely on what you want from your trip — whether that is beach time, cultural exploration, hiking, business travel, or simply escaping the cold.

Cyprus enjoys one of the warmest climates in the Mediterranean. Paphos and Larnaca each average roughly 3,350–3,400 hours of sunshine a year, and winters are mild: average daytime highs on the coast are around 17°C even in January, although nights can drop to 7–9°C. Temperatures below are average daytime highs on the coast from the Cyprus Department of Meteorology's long-term records; inland Nicosia is several degrees hotter in summer and cooler in winter. But the island's character shifts dramatically with the seasons, and each month brings its own combination of weather, events, pricing, and crowd levels.

As a Cyprus-based, IATA-accredited travel agency (Tourism Licence 7775) that has been advising travellers since 2006, we have a detailed understanding of how the island performs throughout the year. This month-by-month guide gives you everything you need to choose the perfect time for your visit.

## January

**Weather:** Cool, with some of the year's wettest days (coastal highs around 17°C, nights around 7–9°C). Snow on Mount Olympus in the [Troodos Mountains](https://www.visitcyprus.com/index.php/en/discovercyprus/rural/troodos-region), where temperatures can drop below freezing.

**Crowds:** Very low. This is one of the quietest months on the island.

**Pricing:** The lowest prices of the year for flights and accommodation. Excellent value for budget-conscious travellers.

**What to do:** Skiing and snowboarding on Mount Olympus (Cyprus's only ski area) when there is enough snow — in 2026 the first slope opened on 14 January. Exploring archaeological sites like Kourion and Paphos without crowds. Enjoying cosy taverna dinners in mountain villages.

**Best for:** Budget travellers, cultural explorers, skiers (snow permitting), and those who want to experience Cyprus's quieter, more authentic side.

## February

**Weather:** Similar to January (coastal highs around 17°C) but with slightly more sunshine. Almond trees begin to blossom, bringing a beautiful pink-white blanket to the countryside.

**Crowds:** Low, with a spike during Carnival celebrations.

**Pricing:** Still low season pricing, though Carnival week may see slight increases in Limassol hotels.

**What to do:** **Limassol Carnival** — one of the biggest events on the Cypriot calendar. Almost two weeks of parades, costume parties, music, and street food, culminating in the Grand Carnival Parade through central Limassol (in 2026 the programme ran from 10 to 22 February). It is a genuinely exciting time to visit, with a festive atmosphere that draws locals and visitors alike. The dates follow Orthodox Easter: Carnival ends the day before Clean Monday, which was 23 February in 2026 and falls on 15 March in 2027, so in 2027 the parades move into March.

**Best for:** Carnival lovers, cultural travellers, and anyone who enjoys festivals and community celebrations.

## March

**Weather:** Spring arrives. Coastal highs rise to around 19–20°C. Wildflowers begin to carpet the hillsides and meadows, particularly in the Akamas Peninsula and around Paphos.

**Crowds:** Still relatively low, though the first tourists begin to appear.

**Pricing:** Transitional pricing — still below peak but starting to rise as demand increases.

**What to do:** Hiking in the Akamas Peninsula and Troodos Mountains (wildflower season is spectacular). Visiting Paphos's Tombs of the Kings and the mosaics of the Archaeological Park. The **TUI Cyprus Marathon** is run in Paphos in late winter (7–8 March in 2026; 27–28 February in 2027).

**Best for:** Hikers, nature lovers, and photographers. One of the most beautiful months in Cyprus.

## April

**Weather:** Warm and pleasant (coastal highs of 21–23°C). The sea is still cool for swimming (around 18°C) but the weather on land is ideal for outdoor activities.

**Crowds:** Moderate. Greek Orthodox Easter (if it falls in April) brings a domestic surge, but international tourism is still building. Orthodox Easter Sunday was 12 April in 2026; in 2027 it falls on 2 May.

**Pricing:** Shoulder season pricing — good value for quality.

**What to do:** **Greek Orthodox Easter** — the most important religious and cultural event in Cyprus. Experience midnight church services, candlelit processions, the cracking of red-dyed eggs, and the feast of souvla (spit-roasted lamb) that follows Easter Sunday. Every village celebrates, and the atmosphere is warm and welcoming.

**Best for:** Cultural travellers, families, and anyone who wants to experience authentic Cypriot traditions.

## May

**Weather:** Warm and sunny (coastal highs of 24–27°C) and almost dry. The sea warms to around 20°C. Long days: by the end of May there are over 14 hours of daylight, with sunrise before 6 AM and sunset just before 8 PM.

**Crowds:** Moderate to busy. The summer tourist season is beginning, but it is not yet at peak intensity.

**Pricing:** Shoulder to high season pricing. Still good value compared to June–August, especially for hotel bookings.

**What to do:** Beach season begins in earnest. Water sports, boat trips, and coastal exploration are all at their best. Wine villages in the Commandaria region offer tastings and vineyard tours.

**Best for:** Beach lovers, water sports enthusiasts, and couples. Arguably the best overall month to visit Cyprus — warm enough for the beach (if you don't mind a cool first swim), comfortable for exploring, and not yet overcrowded.

## June

**Weather:** Hot and dry (coastal highs of 28–31°C, mid-30s inland). The sea reaches around 24°C. Virtually no rain. Long, sunny days.

**Crowds:** Busy. International tourist arrivals increase significantly. Beaches and popular sites become noticeably more crowded.

**Pricing:** High season pricing begins. Book flights and hotels well in advance for the best rates.

**What to do:** **Kataklysmos (Festival of the Flood)** — a uniquely Cypriot celebration held 50 days after Greek Orthodox Easter (1 June in 2026; 21 June in 2027). Coastal cities, especially Larnaca, host waterfront festivals with boat races, water games, live music, poetry competitions, and traditional food stalls. In Limassol, the annual **MEDFEST** beer festival takes place in late June (24–28 June in 2026).

**Best for:** Beach holidays, water sports, cultural festivals, and family travel.

## July

**Weather:** Peak summer heat (coastal highs of 30–33°C; inland Nicosia averages about 37°C, and heatwaves can push it above 40°C). The sea is warm (around 26–27°C) and ideal for swimming and water sports. Dry and cloudless.

**Crowds:** Peak season. Beaches, restaurants, and tourist sites are at their busiest. Expect queues at popular attractions and full hotels.

**Pricing:** Peak pricing across the board. Book 2–3 months in advance for the best availability.

**What to do:** Beach life dominates. Ayia Napa and Protaras offer the most vibrant beach scene, and Fig Tree Bay in Protaras is one of the island's best-known beaches. Sunset boat cruises, scuba diving, and jet skiing are all popular. Evening entertainment thrives in resort areas.

**Best for:** Beach holidaymakers, families during school holidays, and those who love warm weather and nightlife.

## August

**Weather:** As hot as July (coastal highs of 30–33°C, around 37°C inland, higher in heatwaves). The sea is at its warmest, around 27–28°C. Dry and intensely sunny — sunscreen, hats, and hydration are essential.

**Crowds:** Peak crowds. August is the busiest month for both international tourists and Cypriot families taking summer holidays.

**Pricing:** Highest prices of the year, particularly for beachfront accommodation and popular resort areas.

**What to do:** Beach and pool days. **Assumption of the Virgin Mary (15 August)** is a major public holiday with religious processions and community celebrations across the island, particularly in mountain villages.

**Best for:** Sun seekers, families, and festival-goers. Be prepared for the heat and plan outdoor activities for mornings and evenings.

## September

**Weather:** Still warm (coastal highs of 29–31°C) but the extreme heat begins to ease. The sea remains warm (around 26–27°C) — some of the best swimming conditions of the year. Occasional light rain towards the end of the month.

**Crowds:** Declining from peak levels. Schools reopen, so family tourism drops significantly. Beaches and restaurants are pleasantly busy rather than overcrowded.

**Pricing:** Shoulder season pricing returns. Excellent value for quality — warm weather, fewer crowds, lower prices.

**What to do:** The **Pafos Aphrodite Festival** stages open-air opera in the square in front of Paphos medieval castle in early September (the 24th festival, Donizetti's *L'elisir d'amore*, was on 4–5 September 2026). Later in the month comes the [Limassol Wine Festival](https://www.visitcyprus.com/) — a long-running annual festival in Limassol's Municipal Gardens, usually from late September into early October (26 September – 4 October in 2026). Nine days of wine tastings, traditional food, concerts, and dancing — a great event for wine lovers and anyone who wants to experience Cypriot culture at its most convivial.

**Best for:** Wine lovers, couples, and value-conscious travellers. September is widely considered one of the best months to visit Cyprus — warm seas, golden light, and a relaxed atmosphere.

## October

**Weather:** Warm and pleasant (coastal highs of 26–29°C). The first autumn rains may arrive, refreshing the landscape. The sea is still comfortable for swimming (around 24–25°C).

**Crowds:** Low to moderate. Tourist numbers drop significantly, creating a more peaceful experience.

**Pricing:** Shoulder to low season pricing. Excellent deals on flights and accommodation.

**What to do:** Hiking season returns — the Troodos Mountains and Akamas Peninsula are gorgeous in autumn, with cooler temperatures and the return of green vegetation. Cultural exploration is at its best with comfortable temperatures and empty archaeological sites.

**Best for:** Hikers, cultural explorers, and travellers who want Mediterranean warmth without summer crowds. Outstanding value.

## November

**Weather:** Mild (coastal highs of 22–23°C) with increasing rainfall. The landscape turns green after the first rains. Sea temperature drops to around 22°C — still swimmable for hardy swimmers.

**Crowds:** Low. Tourism is at near-minimum levels.

**Pricing:** Low season pricing and some of the best hotel deals of the year, although many seasonal resort hotels close for part of the winter, so the choice of beach hotels narrows.

**What to do:** The olive harvest in the villages. Mushroom foraging in the Troodos forests after the first rains. Cosy evening dining in traditional tavernas without needing reservations.

**Best for:** Budget travellers, hikers, food enthusiasts, and anyone who wants to experience everyday Cypriot life without the tourist veneer.

## December

**Weather:** Cool (coastal highs of 18–19°C); December and January are the wettest months. Snow usually starts to fall on the higher Troodos before Christmas.

**Crowds:** Low, with a bump around Christmas and New Year.

**Pricing:** Low season, with modest increases during the holiday week (23 December – 2 January).

**What to do:** Christmas markets and festive events in Limassol, Nicosia, and Paphos. Mountain villages are atmospheric with wood-fire smoke, seasonal treats (melomakarona, kourabiedes), and a genuine festive spirit. The Mount Olympus ski slopes open only once there is enough snow cover — in the 2025/26 season the first slope opened on 14 January.

**Best for:** Budget travellers and Christmas atmosphere seekers. A surprisingly charming time to visit Cyprus.

## Best Months by Activity

| Cultural Exploration | Year-round (best: March – May, September – November) |

| Wine Tourism | Late September – early October (Limassol Wine Festival), year-round for vineyard visits |

| Budget Travel | November – March |

## Peak vs Shoulder vs Off-Season Summary

| Season | Months | Weather | Crowds | Price Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Peak** | July – August | Hot (30–33°C on the coast, hotter inland) | Very busy | Highest |
| **Shoulder** | April – June, September – October | Warm (21–31°C) | Moderate | Good value |
| **Off-season** | November – March | Mild (17–23°C) | Low | Best value |
