## Why Put Your Travel Policy in Writing

*Updated October 2026.* A corporate travel policy is not bureaucracy; it makes the month-end close simpler. Without a written policy, the same problems tend to come back: VAT on travel invoices handled differently each time, cabin classes that drift upwards, no clear plan when something goes wrong on a trip, and the same questions answered again and again by email. A short written policy deals with all four.

This guide sets out what a Cyprus company's travel policy should cover: class-of-travel rules, daily allowances, VAT and invoice requirements, duty of care and a simple approval workflow. It is a template, not a market survey. Every figure in [square brackets] is an example for you to replace with your own policy choice. Where we quote a law or official guidance, we name the source; those sources were checked in October 2026.

## What Every Cyprus Corporate Travel Policy Should Contain

Twelve sections, in the order they should appear in the document:

1. **Scope and definitions** — who the policy covers, which trips it applies to (domestic vs international, booked how), and terms used throughout.
2. **Booking channel** — who is authorised to book (the travel agent, a specific platform, or both), and what is not allowed (personal credit cards, unsanctioned booking sites).
3. **Class of travel** — flight class by flight time and seniority; train and ground transport rules.
4. **Accommodation standards** — star rating limits, preferred hotels, maximum nightly rates by city.
5. **Per-diem and meal allowances** — your daily rates, receipt rules, and the alcohol policy.
6. **Ground transport** — airport transfers, car rental class, taxi vs ride-share rules.
7. **Booking approval workflow** — who approves what, under which thresholds, and the exception path.
8. **VAT and invoicing requirements** — invoices in the company's name with its VAT number, documents for EU VAT refund claims, receipt standards.
9. **Expense submission rules** — deadlines, receipt requirements, reimbursement timeline.
10. **Duty of care** — 24/7 emergency contact, traveller tracking, insurance, medical and evacuation cover.
11. **Policy exceptions** — how to request, who approves, how documented.
12. **Compliance and reporting** — how adherence is measured and what happens when it is not.

## Class of Travel Thresholds — Example Rules to Adapt

Set these too generously and you pay for business class on short flights where economy would do. Set them too tightly and senior staff arrive tired for an important meeting after an overnight flight in economy. A middle position to start from, with example thresholds in brackets:

| Scheduled flight time | Junior / mid-level | Senior / executive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under [e.g. 3] hours | Economy | Economy |
| [e.g. 3–5] hours | Economy | Economy, or premium economy where offered |
| Over [e.g. 5] hours | Premium economy where offered | Business |
| Overnight flight with a meeting on arrival | Premium economy where offered | Business |

Say in the policy whether the threshold applies to the longest single flight or to the whole journey, including connections.

Train rules (example): standard class within Europe for all employees, with an exception for overnight sleepers. Ground transport (example): standard taxi or ride-share for everyone, with a pre-booked driver for senior travellers arriving [e.g. after 23:00] from a long-haul flight.

## Per-Diem and Meal Allowances — Set Your Own Rates

Daily allowances are an easy place for a policy to drift. If employees feel the rate is too low, they claim the gap back with receipts; if they find it generous, they keep the difference. A clear rate that you review regularly deals with both.

This template does not give city rates: meal and transport prices change, exchange rates move, and the right rate depends on who travels and why. Set your own, based on your recent expense claims and current prices in the cities your staff visit, and list them in a table like this:

| City | Standard daily allowance | Senior / client entertainment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [City] | [€ your rate] | [€ your rate] |
| [City] | [€ your rate] | [€ your rate] |
| [City] | [€ your rate] | [€ your rate] |

Write down what the allowance covers (for example meals, drinks and minor local transport) and what it does not.

- **Receipts (example rule):** costs above [your threshold, e.g. €25] need a receipt; smaller costs are covered by the daily allowance. To recover VAT you generally need a valid VAT invoice, whatever the amount.
- **Alcohol (example rule):** reimbursed only as part of client entertainment, up to [your cap per person per client dinner].
- **Tax:** before you set the rates, ask your tax adviser how Cyprus income tax and social insurance treat fixed daily allowances compared with reimbursing receipted costs.

Review the rates at least once a year.

## VAT Treatment — Summary Only

VAT on business travel is simple to describe and fiddly in practice. For the policy, the summary is as follows (checked October 2026):

- **VAT charged in Cyprus** (local hotels, restaurants, taxis): deducted through your Cyprus VAT return under the normal rules, provided you hold a valid invoice. Not every cost qualifies. Under EU VAT law, VAT is never deductible on spending that is not strictly for business, "such as that on luxuries, amusements or entertainment", and each country may keep further blocked categories (VAT Directive 2006/112/EC, Article 176). Recovery also depends on your own business: VAT can only be deducted to the extent the costs are used for your taxed transactions (Article 168), so companies that mainly make VAT-exempt supplies, as many financial services firms do, may recover only part of it or none. Agree the details with your accountant.
- **VAT charged in other EU countries** (for example hotels in Athens, Frankfurt or Paris, or car hire): this cannot go on your Cyprus VAT return, but it can be reclaimed from the other country through the EU electronic refund procedure under Directive 2008/9/EC, which replaced the old "Eighth Directive" procedure from 1 January 2010. You file the claim online with your own tax authority, the Cyprus Tax Department, which forwards it. The deadline is 30 September of the year after the expense (Article 15). What can be refunded is decided by the VAT rules of the country that charged the VAT (Article 5), so VAT on hotels and meals is refundable in some countries and not in others. Source: [European Commission — VAT refunds](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/taxation/vat/vat-directive/vat-refunds_en).
- **Travel bought from a travel agent or tour operator acting in its own name** (for example a flight-and-hotel package): the EU special VAT scheme for travel agents can apply. Under it, VAT that hotels and other suppliers charged the agent for the traveller's benefit cannot be deducted or refunded in any EU country, and the invoice is marked "Margin scheme — Travel agents" (VAT Directive, Articles 306, 310 and 226). The scheme does not apply where the agent acts only as an intermediary. If VAT recovery matters, ask before booking how the hotel will be invoiced.
- **VAT charged outside the EU** (for example in the UK since Brexit): EU refund rules do not apply. Some countries run their own refund schemes for foreign businesses, with their own conditions and deadlines. The UK is one example ([HMRC guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-refunds-for-non-eu-businesses-visiting-the-uk), checked October 2026); others have none. The US has sales taxes, not VAT. Ask your accountant whether a claim is worth the paperwork.

The policy itself needs only one paragraph: all invoices are issued in the company's full legal name, with its address and VAT number, and travellers ask hotels for a full VAT invoice in the company's name at check-out. The rest is detail for your finance team and accountant.

## Duty of Care — What Cyprus Employers Should Cover

Cyprus's Safety and Health at Work Law of 1996 (Law 89(I)/1996, as amended, including by Law 74(I)/2026) puts a general duty on employers. In the words of the Department of Labour Inspection, "every employer has a duty to ensure the health, safety and welfare of his/her workers" ([Department of Labour Inspection](https://www.mlsi.gov.cy/mlsi/dli/dliup.nsf/All/923BAB9EED17C38FC2257DDD00274C89), checked October 2026). Treat work travel as part of that duty and include it in your risk assessment. For how far the duty reaches on a particular trip abroad, ask your legal adviser. At a minimum, a policy should state:

- **24/7 emergency contact:** a named person (or the travel agent's 24/7 desk) reachable outside business hours in case of flight cancellation, medical incident, or political disruption.
- **Traveller tracking:** you should be able to tell quickly which employees are in which country. In practice, a booking log from your travel agent plus a shared calendar is often enough. Travel and location data about employees is personal data under the GDPR, so tell staff what you record and why, keep it to what you need, and limit who can see it (GDPR, Article 5).
- **Travel insurance:** corporate travel insurance with medical and evacuation cover for everyone on a business trip, at a minimum level you agree with your insurer or broker [your minimum cover]. Compare single-trip and annual multi-trip policies against how often your staff travel.
- **Visa and entry compliance:** employees check visa, vaccination and entry requirements before booking. A travel agent can help flag requirements, but entry and visa decisions are made by the authorities of the destination country. As of October 2026, Cyprus is not in the Schengen Area; it expects to join in 2027, but that needs a decision by the Council of the EU. Until then, non-EU staff who need a Schengen visa because of their nationality still need one, even if they live in Cyprus. See our [Cyprus and Schengen status page](/en/schengen-cyprus-2026).
- **High-risk destination rules:** a written escalation path for travel to countries covered by Cyprus Ministry of Foreign Affairs travel advice. The ministry also advises Cypriot citizens to register on its connect2cy platform before travelling, so it can contact them in an emergency ([Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://mfa.gov.cy/cyprus-citizens-abroad-and-services-to-the-public), checked October 2026). Staff of other nationalities should check what their own country offers.

"We will handle it when something happens" is not a duty-of-care policy; it is a risk. Put it in writing.

## Booking Approval Workflow — A Simple Decision Tree

Long approval matrices tend not to be followed. A three-tier rule is easier to use; the amounts below are examples to replace with your own:

- **Under [e.g. €800] total trip cost:** the traveller books directly through the approved channel (the travel agent, or a specific platform). No prior approval required. The line manager is copied on the booking email.
- **[e.g. €800–€3,000] total trip cost:** line manager approval required before booking. Ask your travel agent not to issue tickets without the approval email.
- **Above [e.g. €3,000] total trip cost, or any premium-cabin flight, or any trip over [e.g. five] nights:** department head approval required.

Exceptions (e.g. urgent same-day travel, sold-out preferred hotels at a required location) are approved by the named "travel policy owner" — typically the COO, CFO, or Head of Operations.

## Expense and Receipt Requirements — Invoice Basics

For an expense to be reimbursed and its VAT recovered, travellers need a full VAT invoice in the company's name, not a till receipt in their own name. Under EU VAT rules, a full invoice shows (VAT Directive, Article 226; [European Commission — Invoicing](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/taxation/vat/vat-businesses/vat-invoicing_en), checked October 2026):

- The date of issue and a unique sequential number.
- The supplier's full name and address, and its VAT number.
- Your company's full name and address.
- A description of what was supplied (itemised, not just "dinner").
- The VAT rate and the VAT amount.

Your company's VAT number is legally required on the invoice only in some cases, for example when your company itself is liable for the VAT, but it is good practice to ask for it every time.

For hotels, "please invoice this to [Company Name], VAT [number], at [address]" should be standard at check-in. For restaurants, ask for a VAT invoice when you ask for the bill, not afterwards. Train your travellers to do this in their first week; it saves your finance team time later.

Example deadlines: receipts submitted within [e.g. 10] working days of the trip ending; reimbursement within [e.g. 15] working days of a complete claim.

## How to Roll Out a Policy — A 30-Day Plan

A suggested timeline; adjust it to your company:

- **Days 1–5:** Draft the policy using this template as a starting point. Circulate it to the COO/CFO and two frequent travellers for reality-check feedback.
- **Days 6–10:** Finalise it and have your legal adviser review it, especially anything that touches employment terms.
- **Days 11–20:** Announce it to all staff with a written summary, a short all-hands walk-through, and a deadline for questions.
- **Days 21–30:** Switch on the new booking channel; all trips from Day 30 onward are booked under the new policy.

A common mistake is rolling out a policy without switching the booking channel at the same time. People keep booking on consumer sites if the new policy does not close that door.

## FAQ

### Is this policy template legally binding?

A policy is not a substitute for a contract; it is a document your employment contracts and staff handbook can point to. How binding it is depends on how they refer to it, so have your legal adviser review it before rollout.

### Can we customise the template for our firm?

Yes — the template is designed to be edited. Every figure in brackets is an example to replace: daily allowances, approval thresholds, cabin-class limits and preferred suppliers. Keep the twelve-section structure and the duty-of-care and VAT clauses, adapted with your advisers.

### Do we need separate policies for different departments?

Usually no. One policy with seniority bands handles most of what separate departmental policies would address. Exceptions: firms with very specialised travel (e.g. field engineers, on-site consultants) sometimes add a supplementary annex rather than a separate policy.

### How often should we update the policy?

Review it at least once a year. Daily allowances, the preferred supplier list and approval thresholds go out of date fastest. Class-of-travel rules and duty-of-care clauses change less often, but check them whenever the law, your insurance or your travel pattern changes.
