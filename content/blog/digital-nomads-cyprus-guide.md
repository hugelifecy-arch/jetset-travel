## Why Remote Workers Consider Cyprus

Cyprus is an EU member state with a Mediterranean climate, widely spoken English and a time zone (Eastern European Time) that overlaps with working hours in both Europe and the Middle East. For remote workers from outside the EU, it also runs a dedicated residence scheme for digital nomads.

At JetSet Travel, an IATA-accredited agency (Tourism Licence 7775) based in Paphos, we arrange flights and onward travel for people who live and work on the island. This guide sets out the official rules of the digital nomad scheme as of October 2026, with links to the government sources, plus practical points on housing, workspace, connectivity and travel. Rules can change, so always check the official pages before you apply.

## The Cyprus Digital Nomad Visa

The "Cyprus Digital Nomad Visa" scheme is run by the **Migration Department of the Deputy Ministry of Migration and International Protection**. Despite its name, it is a temporary residence permit that you apply for after you arrive in Cyprus. The rules and figures in this section come from the Migration Department's [scheme page](https://www.gov.cy/mip-md/en/documents/digital-nomads-and-family-members/) and its [list of accompanying documents](https://www.gov.cy/app/uploads/sites/174/2025/12/Accompanying-Documents_VIS-Digital-Nomad_19032025.pdf) (version of 19 March 2025), as of October 2026.

The scheme was introduced by a Council of Ministers decision of 15 October 2021. As of October 2026, the Migration Department states that it is accepting applications again and that the ceiling remains **500 permits** (raised from 100 on 3 March 2022).

Meeting the conditions does not guarantee a permit: approval remains at the discretion of the Cyprus authorities.

### Eligibility Requirements

As of October 2026, the Migration Department lists these conditions. You must:

- Be a national of a country **outside the EU and the EEA** (EU, EEA and Swiss citizens do not need this permit; see below)
- Be able to work remotely using telecommunications technology
- Work for an employer registered **outside Cyprus**, or be self-employed with clients **outside Cyprus**
- Have stable and sufficient **net income of at least €3,500 a month**, after deduction of contributions and taxes. The required amount rises by **20% for a spouse or partner** and by **15% for each child**
- Hold **health insurance** covering inpatient and outpatient care and the transport of remains (called "Plan A" on the document list)
- Sign an affirmation that neither you nor your family members will work for, or provide services to, employers or clients established in Cyprus

### Family Members

Your spouse or civil partner and your under-age children can live in Cyprus for the same period as you. Each family member needs their own temporary residence permit, and family members **may not work or carry on any economic activity** in Cyprus.

### Duration, Renewal, Fees and Timing

As of October 2026, according to the Migration Department:

- **First permit:** valid for one year
- **Renewal:** possible for a further two years; apply at least one month before your permit expires
- **When and where to apply:** within three months of arriving in Cyprus, at the Department's central offices in Nicosia, in person or through an authorised representative. If your nationality needs a visa to enter Cyprus, you must obtain it before you travel
- **Fees:** €70 to issue or renew the permit, plus €70 for registration in the Aliens' Registry on first registration
- **Examination time:** 5–7 weeks
- **Biometrics:** your photo and fingerprints are taken at the Department in Nicosia or at the Police Aliens and Immigration unit of your district

While Cyprus remains outside the Schengen Area, residence permits issued by Cyprus are valid only for Cyprus, according to the [Republic of Cyprus visa information](https://www.gov.cy/en/information/visas/). If your nationality needs a Schengen visa, a Cyprus permit does not change that for trips to Greece or other Schengen countries. See our [Schengen visa guide for Cyprus residents](/en/blog/schengen-visa-guide-cyprus-residents) and the [current status of Cyprus and Schengen](/en/schengen-cyprus-2026). If you need a visa to travel to Cyprus in the first place, our [visa services](/en/visa-services) page explains how we can help.

### Required Documents

As of October 2026, the official document list (version of 19 March 2025) asks first-time applicants for:

- A copy of a passport valid for at least three months from the date of application, and a copy of the page showing your last entry into Cyprus
- **Employees:** an employment contract of indefinite duration or at least as long as the permit, the employer's confirmation that you can work remotely and that the work is not for an employer registered in Cyprus, a stamped copy of the company's foreign registration certificate, the company's contact details and your position in it
- **Self-employed:** project or service contracts lasting at least as long as the permit, and your confirmation that you work remotely and not for clients established in Cyprus
- Proof of salary payment showing your net income, and detailed bank statements for the last six months (if you cannot prove your net income, an affirmation is required)
- An original criminal record certificate from your country of origin, or from your country of residence if you live elsewhere
- Original blood test results (HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B and C) and a chest X-ray for tuberculosis, certified by a specialist doctor
- The health insurance certificate (Plan A)
- A title deed or rental agreement for your home in Cyprus, with a matching utility bill
- The affirmation that you and your family will not work for employers or clients in Cyprus

All accompanying documents must be officially translated and duly certified, and the Department does not accept incomplete applications. Renewals need a shorter list. Check the current list on the Migration Department's website before you apply, as it can change.

### Tax Implications

As of October 2026, the Migration Department's scheme page states that permit holders who spend more than **183 days** in Cyprus in the same tax year are considered Cyprus tax residents, provided they are not tax residents of any other country.

Cyprus tax law also has a separate **60-day rule**. According to [PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/cyprus/individual/residence) (last reviewed 4 August 2026), all of these conditions must be met in the tax year: you spend at least 60 days in Cyprus; you do not reside in any other single country for more than 183 days in total; you carry on a business in Cyprus, are employed in Cyprus or hold an office with a Cyprus tax-resident company, and that role does not end during the year; and you keep a permanent home in Cyprus, owned or rented. From 1 January 2026, the former condition of not being tax resident in any other country no longer applies. Because the digital nomad scheme does not allow work for Cyprus employers or clients, whether the 60-day rule can apply to you depends on your circumstances.

Tax residency affects what you pay and where, and it interacts with your home country's rules and any double tax treaty. Take advice from a Cyprus-based tax adviser before you move, and see the [Cyprus Tax Department's guidance on tax residency](https://www.gov.cy/mof-tax/en/documents/tax-residency-domicility/).

### For EU, EEA and Swiss Citizens

EU, EEA and Swiss citizens do not need the digital nomad permit. As of October 2026, the Migration Department's [registration page](https://www.gov.cy/mip-md/en/documents/e-u-e-e-a-citizens-and-family-members-2/e-u-e-e-a-citizens-family-member/registration-of-e-u-citizens-and-members-of-their-families-who-are-also-e-u-e-e-a-citizens-meu1/) says you can stay for up to three months with a valid identity card or passport. To stay longer, you must either be working or self-employed in Cyprus, or have sufficient resources and comprehensive sickness insurance cover in Cyprus, and you apply for a **registration certificate (form MEU1)** within four months of entering the country.

## Cost of Living in Cyprus

Living costs in Cyprus vary by city, neighbourhood and season, and they change quickly, so we do not publish a price table. Build your budget from current listings and quotes instead, and make sure it covers:

- **Rent:** compare current listings in each area you are considering
- **Utilities and internet:** air conditioning in summer can push up electricity bills
- **Health insurance:** required for the digital nomad permit
- **Transport:** decide whether you will need a car, and check bus routes and timetables for where you plan to live
- **Workspace:** a coworking membership if you will not work from home
- **Travel:** flights home and weekends away

### Housing Tips

- **Where to look:** online classifieds such as Bazaraki, local Facebook groups and estate agents.
- **Start short-term:** a serviced apartment or short-term rental for your first weeks lets you get to know neighbourhoods before you sign a lease. Keep the permit timetable in mind: the application is due within three months of arrival and needs a title deed or rental agreement with a utility bill.
- **Read the lease:** length, deposit, notice period and who pays the utilities vary between landlords, so get everything in writing.
- **Furnished or unfurnished:** ask what is included and check the inventory before you sign.
- **Check the internet:** see the connectivity tips below.

## Coworking Spaces in Cyprus

Coworking spaces and serviced offices operate in the larger towns, with more choice in the bigger cities. Spaces open, close and change their terms often, so we do not list individual operators. Before you commit:

- Try a day pass and test the Wi-Fi at the times you will actually work
- Check opening hours, weekend access, and whether meeting rooms or call booths are included
- Ask about the contract length and notice period

## Internet Quality and Connectivity

Reliable internet is essential, and quality can vary from one building to the next, so check before you sign a lease:

- **Fixed broadband:** fibre is available in parts of the main towns, but not everywhere. Ask the landlord which provider and plan serve the property, and run a speed test during the viewing.
- **Mobile data:** a local SIM or eSIM with a data plan is a useful back-up if your home connection drops before an important call. Check the mobile operators' coverage maps for your area.
- **Compare plans:** prices and speeds change, so compare current offers from several providers.

## Lifestyle: What to Expect

### Climate

Cyprus has a Mediterranean climate: hot, dry summers and mild winters on the coast, with most rain falling in winter. In the hottest months you will want air conditioning at home and a cool place to work in the middle of the day; spring and autumn are milder. For climate data, see the [Cyprus Department of Meteorology](https://www.dom.org.cy/).

### Safety

As of October 2026, the UK government's [Cyprus travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/cyprus/safety-and-security) says that crime against tourists is not common, but advises sensible precautions such as keeping your passport, money and valuables safe. It also warns that rental companies often ask for your passport as a deposit, which is illegal, and that you should never hand it over. Read your own government's travel advice before you go.

### Expat and Nomad Community

Cyprus has an established international community, including British, Russian-speaking and other European residents. Facebook groups, Meetup events and coworking-space events are common ways to meet other remote workers.

### Food and Dining

Cypriot food is one of the pleasures of island life: meze at a traditional taverna, halloumi, grilled meat and fish, and excellent local fruit and vegetables. Iced coffees such as the freddo espresso and the frappé are popular. Prices vary by area and venue, so we do not quote them here.

### Healthcare

- **Public healthcare:** Cyprus's General Healthcare System ([GeSY](https://www.gesy.org.cy/)) covers people who meet the Health Insurance Organisation's eligibility rules. Check your own status with the Health Insurance Organisation rather than assuming you are covered.
- **Private healthcare:** available across the island, paid for directly or through insurance.
- **Digital nomad permit holders:** must hold the Plan A health insurance described above, for both the first permit and renewals.
- **EU citizens staying longer than three months** without working in Cyprus need comprehensive sickness insurance cover in Cyprus as one of the registration conditions.

Health insurance for a residence permit is not the same as travel insurance. For trips away, and for costs that health cover does not include, such as cancellation, see our [travel insurance guide](/en/blog/travel-insurance-cyprus-visitors-guide).

## Cyprus as a Mediterranean Travel Base

Cyprus is a short flight from Greece and has direct flights to the UK, many European cities and parts of the Middle East, which makes it a practical base for working during the week and travelling at weekends.

### Flights from Cyprus

As of October 2026:

- **Larnaca** has the wider network: many daily flights to Athens, other Greek destinations, the UK, many European cities and the Gulf.
- **Paphos** mainly serves the UK and Europe, and some of its routes run only in summer.
- **There are no flights between the Republic of Cyprus and Turkey.** To reach Istanbul or the Turkish coast, you need to connect through another country.
- **Middle East routes**, including flights to Israel and the Gulf, have been disrupted at times and can change at short notice. Check current schedules and your government's travel advice before you book.

For a side-by-side comparison of the two airports, see our [Larnaca vs Paphos airport guide](/en/blog/larnaca-vs-paphos-airport-2026).

### Weekend Trip Ideas from Cyprus

- **Athens:** many daily flights from Larnaca, for ancient sites, food and lively neighbourhoods.
- **Crete:** Cyprus Airways flies direct between Larnaca and Heraklion on some days of the week; check the current schedule.
- **Santorini:** direct from Larnaca in summer only; at other times, connect in Athens.
- **Mykonos and other Greek islands:** usually via Athens.
- **European cities:** direct flights from Larnaca and Paphos reach many European cities, though some routes are seasonal.

Greece is in the Schengen Area, so if your nationality needs a Schengen visa, you will need one for these trips even while you live in Cyprus. For island-by-island routings, see our [Mediterranean destinations guide](/en/blog/luxury-mediterranean-destinations-2026).

## Getting Started

If you are considering Cyprus as your next base, here is a practical action plan:

1. **Check which route applies to you.** EU, EEA and Swiss citizens register for an MEU1 certificate if they stay longer than three months; non-EU nationals apply for the digital nomad permit.
2. **Read the official document list early.** The criminal record certificate, medical tests and official translations can take time to arrange.
3. **Arrange short-term accommodation first**, but plan to have a rental agreement and utility bill in time for your permit application, which is due within three months of arrival.
4. **Arrange the right health insurance** (Plan A for the permit) and separate travel insurance for trips away.
5. **Set up connectivity:** a local SIM, and broadband checked at the property before you sign.
6. **Consult a tax adviser** to understand your obligations in Cyprus and at home.
