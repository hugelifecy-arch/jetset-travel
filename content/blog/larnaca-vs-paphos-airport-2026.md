## Two Airports, One Island

Almost every trip to or from Cyprus starts with the same practical question: **Larnaca or Paphos?** The island has two international airports, about 130 km apart by road (roughly an hour and a half's drive), and the right answer depends on where you are staying, which airline you are flying, what time of year it is, and — increasingly — what the fare difference looks like once bags and transfers are added in.

At JetSet Travel we book flights out of both airports. As a Paphos-based, IATA-accredited agency (Tourism Licence 7775, IATA 14200130), we have spent 20 years watching clients pick the wrong airport for the right reasons — usually because they compared headline fares and forgot the 90-minute transfer at the other end. This guide sets out how we actually make the choice for our clients in 2026.

## The Short Answer

- **Staying on the west coast** (Paphos town, Coral Bay, Latchi, Polis) → fly **Paphos (PFO)** whenever a route exists.
- **Staying east** (Larnaca, Ayia Napa, Protaras) or in **Nicosia** → fly **Larnaca (LCA)**, no contest.
- **Staying in Limassol** → genuinely either. The drive is roughly 45–60 minutes to each, so choose on schedule and fare.
- **Connecting to the Gulf, the Middle East, or beyond Europe** → **Larnaca**, which carries nearly all of the island's long-haul-facing traffic.
- **Hunting the cheapest low-cost fare from the UK or central Europe in summer** → check **Paphos** first.

Now the detail behind those rules.

## Larnaca and Paphos at a Glance

Both airports are operated by [Hermes Airports](https://www.hermesairports.com/), and 2025 was a record year for them: 13.75 million passengers combined, with 60 airlines flying 160 routes to 41 markets, according to Hermes. Traffic in 2026 has run slightly behind that record (January–August was about 3% lower, which Hermes puts down to geopolitical disruption in the region), with 54 airlines serving 165 destinations in 42 countries as of September 2026.

## Where Each Airport Flies

### Larnaca (LCA): the bigger network

Larnaca handled just over 70% of the two airports' passengers in 2025 and is where the network depth is. It is home base for Cyprus Airways and TUS Airways, hosts a Wizz Air base, and has the frequency that matters for flexible travel: multiple daily Athens rotations with Aegean, daily London Heathrow with British Airways, and the island's Middle East connectivity — Tel Aviv, Beirut, Amman, and the Gulf hubs of Dubai (Emirates, Cyprus Airways) and Doha (Qatar Airways) that open up one-stop routings to Asia, Africa and Australia. Middle East services were disrupted by the regional conflict in early 2026, so check the latest schedule before you build a trip around one.

If your journey involves a connection — Larnaca is usually the right starting point simply because a missed or delayed leg has more recovery options behind it.

### Paphos (PFO): the low-cost specialist

Paphos has a smaller, leisure-led network, but what it does, it does very well. Ryanair runs by far the largest route network there, and the UK coverage is outstanding: Jet2 flies through the winter from Birmingham, East Midlands, Leeds Bradford, London Gatwick, London Stansted, Manchester and Newcastle, with summer flights from several more UK airports, while easyJet flies from London Gatwick, London Luton, Manchester, Bristol and Edinburgh. British Airways flies from London Gatwick, TUI covers the package market, and Ryanair adds a long list of UK, Irish and continental European routes, plus Amman.

The catch is seasonality. Many UK routes continue through the winter, but others are summer-only or pause for part of it — easyJet's Bristol and Edinburgh flights, for example, are scheduled to pause from early January to February 2027 — and several continental routes run in summer only. If you need a specific city pair in January, check whether it actually operates that month before you plan around it — or [ask us](/en/contact) and we will check for you.

## Drive Times That Actually Decide It

For most travellers the deciding factor is not the airline — it is the transfer. Approximate driving times in normal traffic:

| Where you're staying | To Paphos (PFO) | To Larnaca (LCA) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paphos town / Kato Paphos | 20–25 min | 1 h 30–1 h 45 |
| Coral Bay | 30–40 min | 1 h 45–2 h |
| Latchi / Polis | 50–65 min | 2 h 15+ |
| Limassol | 45–60 min | 50–60 min |
| Troodos (Platres) | ~1 h 10 | ~1 h 30 |
| Nicosia | ~1 h 45–2 h | 40–50 min |
| Larnaca town | 1 h 30–1 h 45 | 10–15 min |
| Ayia Napa | ~2 h | 40–50 min |
| Protaras | ~2 h 10 | ~1 h |

Two things stand out. First, **Limassol and the Troodos are the only genuine toss-ups** — everywhere else, one airport wins clearly. Second, a €25 fare saving stops being a saving when it comes with an extra 90 minutes each way and a longer taxi bill: a taxi from Larnaca airport to Paphos typically costs several times more than the short hop from Paphos airport.

Both airports have scheduled shuttle coaches. Limassol Airport Express runs from both airports to Limassol; Kapnos Airport Shuttle links Larnaca airport with Nicosia, Ayia Napa and Protaras, and Paphos airport with Nicosia and Larnaca airport. Local buses run from Larnaca airport into Larnaca town, and from Paphos airport into Paphos (route 612 via Kato Paphos to the Tombs of the Kings bus station, route 613 to Karavella station in the town centre). Shuttles stop at fixed points rather than at hotels, and departures are not continuous, so check the timetable against your arrival time. For families and groups with luggage, a pre-booked private transfer is often the simplest door-to-door option — something we can arrange alongside [flight bookings](/en/flight-tickets-cyprus).

## Fares, Baggage and Disruption

Headline fares out of Paphos are often lower — that is the Ryanair/Jet2/easyJet effect. But compare like with like:

- **Add the bags.** Low-cost hand-luggage fares climb quickly once you add a checked bag each way; a full-service fare into Larnaca with luggage included is sometimes cheaper for a family of four.
- **Add the transfer.** Use the drive-time table above and price the taxi or car hire difference honestly.
- **Think about disruption.** Larnaca's denser schedule means more re-routing options when a flight cancels. From Paphos, on a route that only operates two or three times a week, the "next available flight" may be days away.

This is exactly the comparison we run when clients [request a quote](/en/quote) — both airports, total journey cost, total journey time.

## For Business Travellers

For corporate itineraries the answer is almost always Larnaca: early-morning departures, multiple daily Athens rotations for same-day meetings, the Heathrow service, and one-stop Gulf connections eastbound. If your company is based in Limassol or even western Cyprus, the frequency advantage usually outweighs the marginally longer drive. Our [corporate travel team](/en/corporate-travel) can build airport choice into your travel policy for exactly this reason. For now, Cyprus is still outside Schengen and does not use the EU Entry/Exit System (EES) at Larnaca or Paphos: flights to Schengen countries still involve passport control, and non-EU short-stay visitors are registered in the EES when they land in a Schengen country. Full [Schengen membership](/en/blog/cyprus-schengen-2026-business-travel) is expected in 2027, subject to an EU Council decision; until then, departure formalities at both airports stay as they are.

## Flying Into One, Out of the Other

Open-jaw itineraries — into Larnaca, home from Paphos, or vice versa — are perfectly practical and sometimes the cheapest combination, especially for two-centre holidays (east coast + west coast). The main cost trap is one-way car hire fees between airports; check that before you book, or use a point-to-point transfer for the middle leg.

## How We Choose for Our Clients

Our booking rule of thumb, refined over two decades in [Paphos](/en/paphos-travel-agency):

1. **Geography first.** West of Limassol, default to Paphos; east of Limassol, default to Larnaca.
2. **Then the route.** If only one airport serves your city nonstop in your travel month, that usually settles it.
3. **Then total cost and time** — fare + bags + transfer, door to door, both directions.
4. **Then resilience.** Tight connection, cruise departure or a wedding you cannot miss? Weight Larnaca's frequency, or build in a buffer day.

## FAQ

### Which airport is cheaper to fly into?

Usually Paphos for low-cost point-to-point fares from the UK, Ireland and central Europe — but not always once checked bags and transfers are included. For full-service and Middle East routes, Larnaca is often cheaper simply because the routes exist there. Always price both.

### Which airport should I use for Limassol?

Either — Limassol sits roughly between them, about 45–60 minutes' drive from each. Pick whichever has the better fare and flight time for your dates. Cruise passengers sailing from Limassol port should also check both airports when booking [cruise packages](/en/cruises).

### Can I fly into Larnaca and out of Paphos?

Yes. Open-jaw bookings are routine and often sensible for two-centre holidays. Watch for one-way car hire drop-off fees between the two airports; a pre-booked transfer is often the cleaner solution.

### Is there public transport from the airports?

Yes. Limassol Airport Express runs from both airports to Limassol, and Kapnos Airport Shuttle connects both airports with Nicosia (and Larnaca airport with Ayia Napa and Protaras). Local buses run into Larnaca town and Paphos town, and taxis wait outside arrivals at both airports. For families with luggage, a pre-arranged private transfer is often the most convenient door-to-door option.

### Which airport is better for long-haul connections?

Larnaca. Neither airport has nonstop flights to North America or the Far East, but Larnaca's one-stop options — via London, Athens, Vienna, Dubai and Doha — are far deeper than anything available from Paphos.
