## "Do I Really Need Travel Insurance for Cyprus?"

It is one of the most common questions we hear at JetSet Travel, usually asked in a tone that hopes the answer is no. Cyprus is in the EU, the healthcare is good, and plenty of visitors carry a European Health Insurance Card — so is a policy really necessary?

One thing to be clear about up front: **we are a travel agency, not an insurance company**. Where we arrange cover, we do so as sub-agents of licensed Cyprus insurance providers — the policy itself is always issued and underwritten by the insurer, never by us, and you are always free to buy your cover elsewhere. What follows is the same practical advice we give every client after 20 years of watching trips go right — and occasionally very wrong — as an IATA-accredited agency (Tourism Licence 7775) based in Paphos.

## The Short Answer

- Travel insurance is **not an entry requirement** for visitors who do not need a visa — for example, EU, UK and US citizens.
- It **is required if you need a visa**: as of October 2026, the [Republic of Cyprus visa rules](https://www.gov.cy/en/information/visas/) require travel medical insurance with **at least €30,000 of cover**.
- An **EHIC or UK GHIC is genuinely useful in Cyprus — but it is not travel insurance**, and the gap between the two is where the expensive surprises live.
- Our advice to every client, without exception: travel with a proper policy. It exists for the two costs that can be very large — **medical repatriation and trip cancellation**.

## When Insurance Is Required: Visa Applicants

If you need a visa to visit Cyprus, insurance stops being a recommendation. As of October 2026, the Republic of Cyprus [visa information page](https://www.gov.cy/en/information/visas/) lists travel medical insurance as a required document: minimum cover of €30,000 for each applicant, covering repatriation for medical reasons, emergency medical care and emergency hospital treatment, for the whole period of stay in Cyprus. The same €30,000 minimum is set for Schengen visas by [Article 15 of the EU Visa Code](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/schengen-borders-and-visa/visa-policy_en). A missing or non-compliant certificate can lead to a refusal; the decision always rests with the consulate.

Cyprus is not yet in the Schengen Area. As of October 2026 it expects to join in 2027, subject to a decision of the EU Council ([current status](/en/schengen-cyprus-2026)). Until then, visas issued by Cyprus are valid only for Cyprus; after full integration, they will also allow travel in the rest of the Schengen Area. Our [visa services team](/en/visa-services) checks insurance certificates as part of every application we prepare.

## What EHIC and GHIC Actually Cover in Cyprus

Cyprus runs a national health system, [GeSY](https://www.gesy.org.cy/), and as an EU member state it is part of the European health card scheme. If you hold an EU-issued [EHIC](https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies-and-activities/moving-working-europe/eu-social-security-coordination/european-health-insurance-card_en) or a UK [GHIC](https://www.nhs.uk/using-the-nhs/healthcare-abroad/apply-for-a-free-uk-global-health-insurance-card-ghic/), you are entitled to **medically necessary state healthcare on the same terms and at the same cost as people insured in Cyprus**. In practice, that means public hospitals and health centres; treatment is not always free, and patient charges (co-payments) can apply.

That is genuinely valuable. But understand the four gaps before you rely on it:

1. **Private care is not covered.** If your hotel or villa calls a private doctor or clinic, the card does not apply. You pay, and without insurance you keep the bill. Where you can, ask whether a provider is public before you are treated.
2. **No repatriation.** Neither EHIC nor GHIC will pay to fly you home. The [UK government's Cyprus travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/cyprus/health) lists medical repatriation to the UK and private healthcare among the costs the cards do not cover, and the [European Commission's EHIC page for Cyprus](https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies-and-activities/moving-working-europe/eu-social-security-coordination/european-health-insurance-card/how-use-card/cyprus-european-health-insurance-card_en) states that the card does not cover transport by air ambulance.
3. **No trip protection.** Cancellation, curtailment, missed departures, lost bags, stolen phones — none of it is health-card territory.
4. **The north is excluded.** The European Commission states that the EHIC is not valid in the northern part of Cyprus. Cross into the north and you are outside the scheme entirely, reliant on private care.

The honest framing: the card is a useful **complement** to travel insurance that can spare you the policy excess for minor state-sector treatment. It is not a substitute.

## The Costs Nobody Budgets For

What does going uninsured actually risk in Cyprus?

- **Private treatment adds up fast.** Private consultations, tests and hospital stays are paid in full by the patient unless insurance covers them.
- **Repatriation is the catastrophic cost.** A medically escorted flight home or an air ambulance can be very expensive, and no health card pays for it. This single item is why insurance exists.
- **The everyday claims are mundane but real:** a missed connection after a delayed inbound flight, a cancelled trip after a family illness, a bag that arrives days late, a car-hire excess after a scrape in a hotel car park.
- **Activity exclusions catch people out.** Some standard policies exclude or restrict quad bikes and scooters, or cover them only with a helmet and an appropriate licence. Divers heading for the Zenobia, the well-known wreck off Larnaca, should check the depth limit on their dive cover: the wreck's deeper sections need more training and experience. Read the activities section before you travel, not after.

## What a Good Cyprus Policy Should Include

The checklist we suggest clients take to a comparison site or their broker:

- **A high emergency medical limit that explicitly includes repatriation** — the repatriation wording is the part that matters.
- **Cancellation cover equal to the full non-refundable cost of your trip** — count flights, hotels and prepaid extras honestly, especially for [luxury itineraries](/en/luxury-travel) where the number is bigger than people assume.
- **Travel delay and missed departure** cover.
- **Baggage and valuables** limits that reflect what you actually carry.
- **Personal liability** cover.
- **An excess you would genuinely be comfortable paying** — check whether it applies per claim and per person, as some cheaper policies carry high excesses.
- **Any activity add-ons you need** — quads, scooters, diving, boat charter.
- **A cruise add-on if you are sailing.** Some policies treat cruising as a separate risk: missed port, cabin confinement and medical evacuation at sea may only be covered with a cruise extension. If you are joining one of the [cruises from Limassol](/en/blog/cruises-from-limassol-2026), this is not optional fine print — see our [cruise booking service](/en/cruises) and we will flag it on your itinerary.

Because Cyprus is in Europe, a European policy is normally enough, and it usually costs less than worldwide cover. The price depends on your age, medical history, trip length and level of cover, so compare a few quotes with the same cover limits.

## Special Cases

- **Families:** some family policies include children at no extra cost — price a family policy against individual policies before buying.
- **Over-65s and pre-existing conditions:** declare everything. An undeclared condition can lead to a claim being refused. Specialist insurers cover this market, so compare them too.
- **Long stays and remote workers:** single-trip policies have a maximum trip length, so check it. If you are wintering in Paphos or working remotely — see our [digital nomad guide](/en/blog/digital-nomads-cyprus-guide) — you need a long-stay or nomad policy, not a stretched holiday one. If you are applying for a residence permit, check the permit's own health insurance requirements separately.
- **Business travellers:** if your employees travel, insurance belongs in the travel policy, not left to each traveller's judgement. Group business travel cover is a duty-of-care item our [corporate travel team](/en/corporate-travel) raises with every managed-travel client.

## For Cyprus Residents Travelling Abroad

The same logic runs in reverse. If you are insured in Cyprus, you can get a Cypriot-issued EHIC. It covers medically necessary state healthcare during temporary stays in other EU and EEA countries and Switzerland, and it can also be used in the UK — and it has exactly the same gaps: no repatriation, no private hospitals, no trip protection. If you travel several times a year, compare an annual multi-trip policy with single-trip policies. And if you are a non-EU resident of Cyprus applying for a Schengen visa, the €30,000 insurance certificate requirement applies to you too — [our visa team](/en/visa-services) can check your certificate.

## FAQ

### Is Cyprus in Europe for travel insurance?

Yes. Cyprus is an EU member state, so it normally falls within a policy's "Europe" area and you do not need worldwide cover. Insurers define their areas differently, though, so check that Cyprus is on your policy's list of countries, and check separately whether northern Cyprus is covered if you plan to cross. Your EHIC or UK GHIC is also valid in the areas controlled by the Republic of Cyprus.

### Is travel insurance worth it for Cyprus?

For most visitors, yes. The EHIC or GHIC covers state healthcare, but not private clinics, medical repatriation or trip cancellation, and those costs can be high. If you need a visa, insurance with at least €30,000 of medical cover is required anyway (as of October 2026, under the Republic of Cyprus visa rules).

### Is travel insurance mandatory to enter Cyprus?

Not for visa-free visitors. It is required for anyone applying for a Cyprus visa: as of October 2026, the application must include travel medical insurance with at least €30,000 of cover.

### Does my EHIC or GHIC work in Cyprus?

Yes — it entitles you to medically necessary state healthcare on the same terms as people insured in Cyprus, in the areas controlled by the Republic of Cyprus; patient charges can apply. It does not cover private clinics, repatriation, or anything in northern Cyprus.

### How much does travel insurance for Cyprus cost?

It depends on your age, medical history, trip length and the level of cover. A European policy is normally enough for Cyprus and usually costs less than worldwide cover. If you travel more than once a year, compare the price of an annual multi-trip policy with two single-trip policies.

### Does travel insurance cover northern Cyprus?

Policies differ: some cover the whole island, others exclude the north. Your EHIC or GHIC is not valid there, so any treatment in the north will be private. If you plan to cross, check your policy wording for territorial exclusions before you travel.

### Do I need special cover for a cruise from Limassol?

Check before you sail. Standard policies may exclude cruise-specific risks — missed port departure, cabin confinement, evacuation at sea — unless you add a cruise extension. Tell your insurer it is a cruise, and tell us so we can sanity-check the itinerary against your cover.
